ROW "held" (8 frames, LOOPS): he is being picked up and held in the air by the viewer, dangling from an invisible grip at the back of his neck/collar — dignified and startled, never slapstick. He stays in profile facing right. This row is an exception to the fixed-feet rule: his feet are OFF the ground and touch nothing. Do NOT draw a hand, a rope, a hook or any other prop — only the character. The head, shoulders and upper chest are the FIXED part: draw them at EXACTLY the same position and size in all 8 frames, as if pinned. The single visible eye is WIDE OPEN throughout — a large round eye with the eyebrow raised high and a small open mouth; his expression does not change between frames. The arms hang loosely down from the shoulders and sway only slightly. The short legs dangle straight down and swing gently left-right like a pendulum: frame 1 centred, frames 2-3 swinging forward (to the right), frame 4 back at centre, frames 5-6 swinging back (to the left), frames 7-8 returning toward centre so that frame 8 leads into frame 1 with a step no larger than the others. The long tail hangs down from his hips and then CURLS BACK UP in a soft S-curve, so that its golden tip ends up beside or behind his legs and NEVER reaches lower than his feet — the tail must not add any height below the feet. It sways in the OPPOSITE direction to the legs, the golden tip trailing last. The overall height of the drawing, from the top of the crown down to the lowest point of the figure, must be about the same as the standing character in the attached idle strip, and the head must be drawn at the SAME size as in that strip — do not draw him smaller. Keep the swing small — no more than about a tenth of his body width in either direction — and make every step the same size, because this row is played on repeat.
