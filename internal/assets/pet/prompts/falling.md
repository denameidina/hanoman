ROW "falling" (8 frames, LOOPS): he has been let go and is drifting downward slowly, bracing himself — calm and dignified, not panicking. He stays in profile facing right and touches nothing; there is no ground, no speed lines, no dust cloud, no motion blur, no props. This row is an exception to the fixed-feet rule: the feet are off the ground. He is TUCKED but still drawn TALL, not curled into a small ball: knees drawn up toward the chest, both arms wrapped around the shins, back rounded, chin tucked, and the long tail RAISED HIGH, curving up and over his back with the golden tip well above the head. The overall height of the drawing, from the raised tail tip down to the tucked feet, must be about the same as the standing character in the attached idle strip — do not draw him smaller and do not draw him larger. The single visible eye is open and steady with the brow slightly drawn, mouth a small firm line; the expression does not change between frames. The head, torso, arms and knees are the FIXED part: draw them at EXACTLY the same position and size in all 8 frames. The only motion is a slow, even sway of the raised tail tip and a gentle lift-and-settle of the loose hem of the sarong: frames 1-4 the tail tip drifts a little to the left and the hem lifts, frames 5-8 they return, with frame 8 leading into frame 1 in a step no larger than the others. Every step must be the same size, because this row is played on repeat.
